Koji, What?

You Already Know Koji. You Just Call It Miso.

That familiar jar in your fridge has a less familiar backstory. Let’s start there.

ai.
Ai Kawahara

Editor · ORYZAE / CTO

November 18, 2026·2 min read

Start in your pantry.

Maybe you know exactly which miso you like. Maybe soy sauce lives next to your stove. Either way, your introduction to koji may have happened long before you learned its name.

Koji plays a part in making both. It is also used in foods and drinks including amazake and sake. Different processes, different results — with a shared culture behind them.1

So, what are we talking about?

Koji is grain or another ingredient cultivated with selected food-use molds. Rice, barley, and soybeans are among the ingredients used. One of the best-known molds involved is Aspergillus oryzae.1

Think of koji as a starting point, rather than a finished flavor. Calling something “made with koji” does not tell you whether it will be salty, sweet, thick, or something you pour over ice. The rest of the process matters.

The little science detour.

As the mold grows, it produces enzymes. In rice-based amazake, enzymes break rice starch down into smaller sugars, helping turn a familiar staple into a sweet drink.2

That is the part that makes us want to pull up a chair. The ingredient has not become unrecognizable. Its possibilities have changed.

One culture. More than one personality.

Your first move? Keep it familiar.

There is no need to reorganize your kitchen. Start with something you already enjoy: a spoonful of miso in a dressing, or a small glass of ready-to-drink rice-based amazake.

Notice the flavor before reaching for a wellness explanation. Is it savory? Rounded? Sweet? How does it work with the rest of what you are eating?

Koji gets more interesting when it becomes something you can taste, rather than another word you feel you ought to know.
