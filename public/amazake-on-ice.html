Sip Check

Amazake, on Ice. Hear Us Out.

A chilled glass, a little citrus, and a different way into a Japanese rice drink.

ai.
Ai Kawahara

Editor · ORYZAE / CTO

November 18, 2026·2 min read

A cold introduction.

You do not need a special occasion for a new drink. Sometimes a clean glass and a few ice cubes are enough.

For this first Sip Check, we are starting with rice-based amazake: a sweet Japanese drink made with rice koji. It can be enjoyed cold as well as warm.1 The serving ideas below are our suggestions for exploring it at home, rather than a traditional recipe.

First, get the right bottle.

Look for rice-based or koji amazake. Another kind of amazake is made with sake lees, and may contain alcohol. The names can look similar, so read the ingredients and alcohol information on the actual bottle.1

Choose a ready-to-drink product for the simplest starting point. If yours is a concentrate, follow its dilution directions first. The ratios below assume a drink that is already ready to pour.

Three ways to find your pour.

01 / Keep it simple

The cold pour

Chill the bottle. Shake or stir if the label calls for it, then pour 100 mL over a few ice cubes. Take a sip before adding anything. This is your starting point.

02 / Brighten it up

The citrus turn

To that same pour, add 1 teaspoon of lemon or lime juice. Stir, taste, and adjust. The idea is to explore a brighter balance, not cover up the drink.

03 / Make a little room

The longer sip

Start with 100 mL of ready-to-drink amazake and 30 mL of cold water. Stir and taste. Add a little more water if you prefer a lighter pour; try the citrus addition here, too.

Sweet is still sweet.

In koji amazake, rice starch is broken down into sugars by enzymes.2 A drink can taste sweet without having table sugar added, but that does not make it sugar-free. Check the nutrition label instead of treating fermentation as a shortcut around it.

Serve a small glass alongside a snack or lunch, and see whether you like it. No grand routine required.

Use commercially prepared amazake and follow its storage and after-opening directions. These serving suggestions do not involve fermenting a drink at home.
